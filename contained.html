Contained-emotion optimization — did it actually work?

Can a voice speak a neutral 20-word sentence so an intense emotion is clearly felt but sounds held back — and can we measure that restraint? Four agents (brain gpt-5.4, judge Gemini) ran 12–16 evolution rounds. Below is the honest read: not single cherry-picked clips, but the group means over every intelligible take whose prompt cue told the model to suppress the emotion vs. to let it out.

What the measurement says — the short version

1. Suppressing the emotion output: this works — most clearly for Amusement (−0.62) and Fear (−0.36): given a "hold it back" cue, the model measurably lowers the emotion it puts into the delivery. That is genuine containment (the feeling is set up, then dialled down).

2. A VoiceNet "tension" fingerprint of suppression: mostly NOT confirmed. The restraint markers (TENS / RESP / COGL) do not reliably separate contained from free — only Fear shows a real TENS rise (+0.14); Anger and Amusement are flat, Sadness only +0.07. So "detect that someone is suppressing an emotion from throat-tension alone" is not demonstrated here. My first version overstated this by using single hand-picked clips; the aggregates below are the fair test.

3. Caveats. The agent explored far more contained than free cues (Anger has only 2 free takes → no real control). Intelligibility caps everything (Fear keeps only 41/160 takes). The Gemini audio-judge returned score 0 on almost every round, so its scores are unreliable — only its written tips are used.

Anger INCONCLUSIVE

The agent almost never tried a 'free' anger cue (only 2 intelligible free takes), so there is no reliable contained-vs-free contrast for anger. What we can say: contained anger keeps a high TENS baseline (~0.46) with a low anger output — but without a free control this isn't proof.

Gemini prompting tips (written feedback; its numeric scores were unreliable)

  • Increase subtle breath pressure and micro-unsteadiness on key stressed vowels without increasing overall volume instability.
  • Introduce slight throat tightness on sentence finishes to ensure the suppressed emotion reads as restrained rather than neutral.
  • The current outputs risk leaning too far into flat, ordinary reading; the suppressed emotional core needs to be audible through subtle pitch micro-tremors and faint breath-catches rather than total flattening.

Contained emotion — can we make restraint measurable? (round 2)

Round 1 showed that "hold it back" prompts just make the emotion quieter (mild), and that VoiceNet tension did not separate contained from free. So we set the hard target and let four agents (gpt-5.4 + Gemini) loose with the full toolbox: keep the emotion strong while driving the restraint markers (TENS/COGL/RESP) measurably higher than a matched free take — by merging VoiceNet restraint LoRAs with the emotion LoRA, not just prompting. 13–18 evolution rounds each, ~1,000–1,450 candidates per emotion.

Verdict: one partial solve (Fear), the rest engine-limited

The mechanism that works (found on Fear): do not suppress the emotion semantically. Keep the emotion LoRA strong and add only tiny surface-channeling adapters (vn_TENS/ATCK/CLRT ≈ 0.05–0.15) so the feeling is forced through a narrower, drier, more pressurized channel. Heavy restraint stacks (0.4–1.1) do the opposite — they collapse the emotion or break intelligibility.

The honest limit: for Amusement (and largely Anger, Sadness) emotional intensity and muscular tension are acoustically entangled in this model — you cannot turn up "held-back tension" independently of the emotion. Only Fear produced a clean, intelligible pair where fear stays high (0.57) and COGL (+0.10) / RESP (+0.14) are clearly higher than free.

Fear PARTIAL SOLVE

The one real win. The trick is not to suppress the fear — keep the same vivid panic, then add only tiny surface adapters so it is forced through a narrower, drier, more pressurized channel. Result: fear stays high (0.57) and COGL/RESP are clearly higher than the matched free take, at clean speech (WER 0.08).

Contained-emotion optimization (agent-discovered)

Can we make a voice speak a neutral 20-word sentence so an intense emotion clearly comes through but sounds held back — as if the speaker is fighting to suppress it — and can we measure that restraint with VoiceNet? Four autonomous research agents (brain: gpt-5.4, judge: Gemini) each ran ~12–16 evolution rounds (population 10, mean-of-8) over the six voice profiles and DE/EN carrier sentences.

How restraint was scored

Fitness = the target emotion + TENS (throat/jaw tension) + COGL (cognitive effort of holding it) + RESP (controlled/held breath), gated by word-error-rate. The contained exemplar below is the take with the strongest restraint signature where the emotion is still present but not maxed; the free exemplar is the most intense intelligible take.

Headline finding: containment = a reduced emotion-LoRA dose (≈0.2–0.3; Fear needs ~0.5) + a suppression prompt cue. The measurable signature of "holding it back" is higher TENS / RESP / COGL while the raw emotion output drops — the feeling shows up as voice-quality tension, not as emotional release. Intelligibility (WER) is the ceiling: push restraint too hard and speech breaks (Fear is the hardest).

Gemini prompting tips for better containment

  • Increase subtle breath pressure and micro-unsteadiness on key stressed vowels without increasing overall volume instability.
  • Introduce slight throat tightness on sentence finishes to ensure the suppressed emotion reads as restrained rather than neutral.
  • The current outputs risk leaning too far into flat, ordinary reading; the suppressed emotional core needs to be audible through subtle pitch micro-tremors and faint breath-catches rather than total flattening.

What is this, in plain language?

The model

MOSS-VA-v2 (laion/moss-tts-local-transformer-4.55b-voice-acting-v2) is a 4.55-billion-parameter text-to-speech model tuned for voice acting: you give it a line of text plus a short written stage direction ("a warm, delighted tone"), and it speaks the line in that manner. It can also clone a voice from a few seconds of reference audio and keep speaking in that person's timbre.

Emotion LoRAs

On top of the base model we trained 40 small emotion adapters ("LoRAs") — one per emotion. Merging an adapter in nudges the voice toward that specific feeling. A dial called λ (lambda) controls how strongly: low λ = a hint of the emotion, high λ = full-blown. Each emotion has its own best λ (found earlier by an evolutionary search).

The 4 delivery conditions

A real actor can play the same feeling in very different ways. We cross two axes: intensity (intense vs. moderate) × expression (freely let out vs. contained/held-back). That gives four conditions: A intense & free, B moderate & free, C intense but contained (the character fights to suppress a strong feeling and it leaks through), D moderate & quietly contained. Intensity sets the LoRA λ and the wording of the stage direction; containment is an extra delivery cue added to the line.

How each clip was chosen

For every voice × emotion × condition cell we generated 32 candidate takes and kept the single best. Ranking = z(genuineness) + z(vocal-burst-blend) + 1.25·emotion-strength (an automatic scorer that rates how genuine and how emotional the voice sounds), and of the top 5 we picked the one whose voice most closely matches the reference speaker (ECAPA speaker similarity). So each of the 960 clips is a best-of-32, speaker-checked take. 0 cells failed — the grid is complete.

Take-aways

1. Intensity trades against fidelity. Higher emotion λ reliably raises felt emotion but lowers both genuineness and speaker similarity — there is no free lunch.
2. Containment is cheap, intensity is expensive. Holding a feeling back keeps the voice natural and on-timbre; cranking intensity is what degrades the clone.
3. Intense-but-contained is the hardest ask — the least natural of the four, useful dramatically but the riskiest to generate.
4. Voice-clone quality is robust to small / noisy references; expression does not need a pristine source clip.
5. Soft positive emotions (gratitude, relief, contentment) render far more strongly than cold negatives (malice, contempt, sourness).
